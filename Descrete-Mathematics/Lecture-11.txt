A Pigeonhole states:	If more n objects are placed into n boxes, then at least one box must contain two or more objects. Ex: Suppose there are 13 people in a room
			but only 12 months in a year. Since birthdays can only occur in only 12 months, at least two people must have birthdays in the same month. 

			Scope:	The pigeonhole principal says that if n+1 objects are placed into n containers, then there must exist a container with at least 2 objects.
				The parity principal allows us to figure out many mathematical conundrums by simply keeping track of odd and even numbers. These can be used
				to prove that certain mathematical constructions are impossible. Parity can be used to provide an effient means of error detection for transmitted
				binary.

				B.	The generalized pigeonhole principal says that pn+1 or more objects are placed into n boxes, then some box has at least p+1 objects.
					1. Proof by contradiction: Suppose that each box has at most p objects. Then the total number of objects would be at most pn, contradicting our assumption.
					2. Ex: Suppose we choose 5 cards from a deck, and there are only 4 suits. In this example at least 1 group has at least 2 cards. Which is ceil(5/4) = 2 or |5,4|=2.
