The Joy of Trigononmetry:
				Quick note before we begin:	Sine (sin) connects an angle to the ratio of the opposite side and the hypotenuse.
								Cosine (cos) connects an angle to the ratio of the adjacent side and the hypotenuse.
								Tangent (tan) connects an angle to the ratio of the opposite side and the adjacent side.

				Trigonometry allows us to calculate areas and measurements often pertaining to triangles that would not be so easily done
				just using the standard techniques of geometry. Trigonometry comes from the Greek trigonometria, the measurement of triangles.
				Trigonometry allows us to calculate triangles using two important functions known as the sine function and cosine function.
				
				We begin with a right triangle with one angle labeled a. The opposite side is called the opposite side. The other side adjacent
				to a that isnt the hypotenuse is called the adjacent side. sine=opposite/hypotenuse(or abbreviated as "sin a")
				The cosine of a (abbreviated as "cos a") is defined as the length of the adjacent side divided by the length of the hypotenuse.
				cosine=adjacent/hypotenuse
				The third most common trigonometric function is the tangent function, which is the sine divided by the cosine. Because the sine
				is opposite/hypotenuse and cosine is adjacent/hypotenuse, the tagent of a(abbreviated as "tan a") is their quotient: tangent=opposite/adjacent.

				Function	Formula			Memory Trick
				Sine		opposite ÷ hypotenuse	SOH
				Cosine		adjacent ÷ hypotenuse	CAH
				Tangent		opposite ÷ adjacent	TOA

				Function	Reciprocal of function	Relationship
				secant		cosine			sec=1/cos
				cosecent	sine			csc=1/sin
				cotangent	tangent			cot=1/tan
